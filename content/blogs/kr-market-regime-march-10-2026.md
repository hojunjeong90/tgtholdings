## A Composite Score of Nearly Zero

The 20-day advance ratio is 30.0%. The percentage of symbols above their 50-day moving average is 36.8% — nearly identical to the 37.1% reading five sessions ago. The breadth acceleration of $+6.32\%$ in the above-50-day-MA ratio over the last twenty-four hours reflects a single day's recovery inside a twenty-day deterioration. The Transition label is earned not by ambiguity but by arithmetic: the layer scores span from $-4.00$ to $+3.00$, and their sum is $-0.08$.

## The Structure of the Transition

The daily path into this Transition is Mean Reversion — the prior session was classified as a mean-reversion environment before the composite recovered to near-zero. The weekly path is also Transition, meaning the intermediate-term regime has not meaningfully shifted. This is a structurally stable Transition: the market is not oscillating between two distinct states. It is oscillating within one.

On 2026-03-05, the composite was $+0.75$. Today it is $-0.08$. The five-session drift is $-0.83$. That drift has occurred despite a 77.7% advance ratio on the final day of that window. The implication is direct: each reflexive bounce in the Korean cross-section is recovering less composite regime score than the prior one. The recoveries are decaying. The Transition regime is not a neutral holding pattern — it is a regime under structural downward pressure that is absorbing upside sessions without confirming a directional reversal.

These readings are not useful as directional signals. They are useful as regime diagnostics. A cross-section where the top five-day return is $+128.5\%$ and the bottom is $-98.8\%$ is a cross-section in which mean-variance optimization assumptions are materially violated. Returns are not drawn from a stable distribution at this dispersion level. Position sizing frameworks that assume normally distributed returns will under-allocate to the extreme losers (correct) and over-allocate to the extreme winners (incorrect — the winners are anomalous, not persistent). The operational implication is to treat names with five-day returns beyond $\pm 50\%$ as regime outliers rather than signal inputs.
